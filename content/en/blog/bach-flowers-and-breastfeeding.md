For a young mother who is breastfeeding, between broken nights, an emotional rollercoaster and new bearings to find, it is not uncommon to turn to gentle support such as **Bach flower essences**. These flower essences, known for their traditional use in managing emotions, raise many questions when considered during breastfeeding: compatibility, choice of format, precautions to know. This guide reviews what is known about their traditional use in this particular context, without replacing medical or pharmaceutical advice.

## What are Bach flower essences?

Bach flower essences are flower preparations developed in the 1930s by Dr Edward Bach, a British physician convinced that certain emotional states deserved a response as gentle as nature itself. His method rests on 38 essences, each traditionally associated with a specific emotion: fear, doubt, loneliness or exhaustion.

Each essence is obtained by macerating flowers in water, then preserved in a small amount of alcohol. The preparations most often come as drops to dilute, but also as spray or pastilles, formats that allow the use to be adapted to each person's preference. This approach belongs to a tradition of emotional wellbeing, distinct from a medical or therapeutic process.

## Bach flower essences during breastfeeding: what to know

The question of compatibility with breastfeeding comes up naturally, as many young mothers look for emotional support that does not disrupt lactation. Bach flower essences do not contain an active substance in the pharmacological sense, which explains why they are often considered during this period. Their use nonetheless remains a personal decision, to discuss if needed with a health professional.

### Why the alcohol-free format is preferred

Classic preparations contain a small amount of brandy, used as a preservative. During breastfeeding, many mothers turn to **alcohol-free** versions, now offered by several manufacturers as drops or spray. Checking the explicit mention on the label remains the simplest guide before any purchase, and a pharmacist can help point towards the most suitable product.

## Common emotions for the young mother who is breastfeeding

The arrival of a child upsets daily bearings, and breastfeeding often comes with a string of emotions that can follow one another within the same day. Fatigue linked to interrupted nights, doubts about the quality of lactation or the bond being built, and sometimes a passing sense of discouragement are among the feelings most often reported by young mothers. These emotional variations, when they remain temporary, are considered a natural reaction to this period of major change.

Some mothers also go through what is commonly called the baby blues, a period of heightened sensitivity in the days following birth. It differs from postpartum depression by its duration, generally limited to a few days, and its more moderate intensity. When the distress persists or intensifies, a consultation remains the approach to favour, as natural approaches cannot replace professional support in that case.

Lack of sleep, often cited as one of the most striking challenges of this period, in turn feeds fatigue and emotional sensitivity. As mentioned in our article on [why babies cry all the time](/en/blog/why-does-my-child-cry-all-the-time/), these moments of intense fatigue for the parent deserve as much attention as the child's own needs.

## Rescue Remedy: occasional support during breastfeeding

Among the best-known preparations is the complex generally called **Rescue Remedy**, a combination of several flower essences intended for moments of occasional tension: a spike of stress, an upset, a particularly difficult night. It is not a long-term treatment but occasional support, traditionally reserved for moments when emotion takes over.

This complex comes in several formats, including alcohol-free spray versions, presented as better suited to pregnancy and breastfeeding. As with any preparation of this kind, reading the label carefully and, in case of doubt, asking a pharmacist remain the most reliable guides.

## How to use Bach flower essences day to day during breastfeeding

The traditional use of Bach flower essences relies on a few drops taken directly under the tongue or diluted in a little water, repeated through the day according to the manufacturer's instructions. The choice of essences is generally made according to the emotion felt at a given moment rather than on the basis of a diagnosis, as each person can react differently to the same preparation.

It is common to combine several essences according to the needs of the moment, without exceeding a limited number of different drops at once, a practice generally recommended by manufacturers to keep a preparation easy to follow. The precise dosage and frequency instructions appear on each product's leaflet and vary from one manufacturer to another, which makes checking them before any use worthwhile.

| Bach flower | Traditional association |
|---|---|
| Olive | Physical and mental exhaustion |
| White Chestnut | Intrusive thoughts, difficulty relaxing |
| Star of Bethlehem | Shock, recent upheaval |
| Pine | Feeling of guilt |
| Walnut | Adapting to a change in life |
| Honeysuckle | Nostalgia for a past period |

## Can Bach flower essences be given to a baby during breastfeeding?

The question of giving Bach flower essences directly to the infant comes up often among young parents, particularly when the baby goes through a phase of agitation or crying that is hard to soothe. This point does not have a general answer: the format, any quantity and even the relevance of this approach for such a young child should be discussed beforehand with a health professional familiar with this type of support, such as a paediatrician or a pharmacist trained in flower essences.

No therapeutic effect can be guaranteed on an infant, and caution remains advisable in the face of information sometimes found without a reliable source. Breastfeeding itself, through the bond it creates between mother and child, is often cited as a source of comfort in its own right.

## Precautions to know before using Bach flower essences

Bach flower essences belong to a tradition of wellbeing rather than a medical approach: no recognised clinical study allows a precise therapeutic effect to be claimed, and their use rests above all on long-standing empirical practice. They in no way replace a prescribed treatment or medical follow-up in case of a persistent condition, whether for the mother or the child.

Distress that settles in over time, marked sadness or difficulty bonding with the baby call for a prompt consultation, just as with any question related to the anxiety discussed in our article on [teenage anxiety](/en/blog/teenage-anxiety/), where professional support also remains the reference in case of lasting distress. A pharmacist also remains the most accessible contact for any question about a product's compatibility with breastfeeding.
