> **In short:**
> 1. Five brands share the **blood circulation** aisle in France, and they do not sell the same thing: HerbalGem bud macerates, Arkopharma and Fleurance Nature whole plant, Super Diet liquid extracts, Granions trace elements.
> 2. Entry pricing starts at 3.99 euros at Arkopharma and climbs to 33.90 euros for a long-course bundle at Granions.
> 3. HerbalGem lines up eleven single buds and five complexes on this need alone, all certified organic and all meant for oral use.
> 4. Four plants structure the whole market: red vine, horse chestnut, ginkgo biloba and witch hazel. The first has held a European monograph since 2010.

## Why product count says nothing about a range

A well-stocked circulation catalogue looks impressive, until you check what sits inside it. On the pages consulted on 20 September 2026, a massage gel, two bottles of essential oil and an infusion share shelf space with supplements meant to be swallowed. Strip out everything that is not taken orally and some catalogues shrink by a third.

The second distortion comes from bundles. A single product sold as a one-month, two-month and three-month pack fills three slots in a collection grid. The Veinomix line shows up several times that way on a page listing only eight items in total.

One question survives all this: how many distinct formulas, genuinely drinkable or swallowable, does the brand offer on this specific need? The answer runs from seven to twenty-one depending on the player, a threefold gap before anything else is even considered.

## Four families of actives behind every label

| Family | Plant example | Usual format | What traditional use points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leaf flavonoids | Red vine | Capsule, ampoule, infusion | Feeling of lightness in the legs |
| Seed saponins | Horse chestnut | Capsule, liquid extract | Tone of the vein wall |
| Bud macerates | Horse chestnut, sweet chestnut, rowan | Drops, spray | Underlying circulation comfort |
| Trace elements | Copper, zinc and related formulas | Ampoule, capsule | General nutritional support |

The first three families overlap heavily from one brand to another. A bottle of red vine from Arkopharma, Super Diet or Fleurance Nature rests on the same botanical species, Vitis vinifera, and on the same part of the plant, the leaf. What changes is the extraction process, the stated dosage and the certification.

The fourth family stands apart. Trace elements are not plants and do not follow the same logic: they supply a nutrient, where a plant extract supplies a set of compounds.

> **What the European framework says**
> A European Medicines Agency monograph has covered the leaf of the red vine since 2010, under traditional use (Vitis vinifera L., folium).

That status has a precise meaning. It certifies a record of use documented well enough to be recognised, and it stops there: no clinical trial demonstration comes with it. No brand, whatever its marketing says, can promise more than this framework allows.

## HerbalGem: betting on the bud rather than the mature plant

The Belgian laboratory, part of the Inula group, has done one thing since the 1980s: bud macerates. That single focus explains the density of its circulation aisle, the deepest of the five brands studied.

Eleven buds are sold individually. Horse chestnut and sweet chestnut on the venous side, rowan for venous return, ginkgo biloba for microcirculation, hawthorn on cardiovascular ground, joined by hornbeam, dogwood, almond, alder, lemon and olive. Five complexes sit alongside them: VenaGem, aimed at the feeling of heavy legs, Cordiagem, positioned on circulation as a whole, and GinkgoGem, centred on one single plant. The laboratory's [full circulation range](https://www.herbalgem.fr/collections/circulation) is certified organic throughout.

Three things set this approach apart from conventional herbal ranges:

- **The part of the plant used.** The bud is picked in spring, before it opens, at a point when the plant concentrates its reserves. Horse chestnut capsules start instead from the mature seed.
- **The way it is taken.** Macerates are counted in drops, diluted in a glass of water, between 5 and 15 a day depending on the reference. Never applied externally: that rule holds across the discipline, with no exception for circulation buds.
- **Pricing consistency.** The gap between the cheapest and the dearest reference in the aisle fits inside eleven euros, where other brands spread their range over twenty euros or more.

The downside is visible straight away: eleven buds for one need is a hard call to make alone. Complexes exist for that reason, and they remain the sensible entry point for a first course.

## What a three-month course actually costs

Comparing bottle by bottle distorts the picture. Brought back to a full quarter of regular intake, the gap between approaches becomes readable:

1. **Powdered plant capsules**: 30 to 45 euros across three months, stringing together entry-level boxes.
2. **Fresh plant extract ampoules**: around 50 euros for the same period, with a ready-measured dose.
3. **Certified organic bud macerates**: 50 to 70 euros, counting roughly one complex bottle a month.
4. **Long-course bundles**: up to 33.90 euros per unit at Granions, a format built to avoid monthly repurchases.

The widest gap comes to about forty euros per quarter between the cheapest option and the most complete one. Spread over a month, the difference stays modest, which explains why price rarely tops the list of selection criteria in this aisle.

## The precautions that apply whatever the brand

Ginkgo biloba and red vine sit among the plants carrying documented precautions during anticoagulant treatment or ahead of surgery. Pharmaceutical or medical advice applies in those cases, whichever brand is picked and whatever the format.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding call for the same caution. Bud macerates also contain a small amount of alcohol, a parameter worth weighing for some people.

One last point falls entirely outside the supplement aisle. A leg swelling on its own, a localised warmth, pain in one calf only: these signs call for a consultation without waiting. The swelling sensation itself also responds to movement and posture, much as when trying to [reduce bloating naturally](/en/blog/reduce-bloating-naturally/), where prolonged sitting shows up among the factors listed.

The simplest measure remains the cheapest: ten minutes of walking after a meal, and legs raised fifteen centimetres at night.
